# Facts used and where they come from

These are textbook astronomy facts. The links are NASA's kids' explainer pages on each topic, listed for reference (not re-checked online while building).

| Claim in the video | Reference |
|---|---|
| Earth spins once a day; the half facing the Sun has day | NASA Space Place, "Why do we have day and night?" — https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/days/ |
| The 24-hour day is a human division of that spin | Convention dating back to ancient Egypt; not a physical property |
| The Moon makes no light; the Sun always lights half of it; phases come from how much of the lit half we see; about one cycle per month (~29.5 days) | NASA Space Place, "Moon phases" — https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/moon-phases/ |
| The 7-day week roughly matches a quarter of the Moon's cycle (~7.4 days); people chose it | Historical convention (Babylonian / Jewish calendar traditions) |
| Earth orbits the Sun once a year; its axis is tilted ~23.4°; the hemisphere leaning toward the Sun has summer | NASA Space Place, "What causes the seasons?" — https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/seasons/ |
| A solar eclipse happens when the Moon passes between the Sun and Earth and its shadow falls on Earth; not every month because the Moon's orbit is tilted (~5°) to Earth's | NASA Space Place, "What is an eclipse?" — https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/eclipses/ |
| Never look at the Sun, even during a partial eclipse; use certified eclipse glasses | NASA eclipse safety — https://science.nasa.gov/eclipses/safety/ |

## Simplifications shown on screen
- Sizes and distances are not to scale (a note is shown on space shots).
- The Moon's orbit tilt in the "no eclipse" shot is exaggerated (~24° drawn vs ~5° real) so the miss is visible.
- In the Day & Night shot the Sun is drawn close to Earth so both fit in one frame; the lighting direction matches where the Sun is drawn.
- Moon phase window shows the view from the Northern Hemisphere (waxing = lit on the right).
- The "Your home" marker is at 31.8°N 35.2°E (`HOME` in `src/main.js`).
