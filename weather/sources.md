# Facts used

| Claim | Source |
|---|---|
| Earth's tilt gives seasons; summer Sun higher and days longer | NASA Space Place, "What causes the seasons?" https://spaceplace.nasa.gov/seasons/ |
| Slanted sunlight spreads over more ground, so each spot gets less energy | same page (flashlight demonstration) |
| Ground loses heat at night; clear skies let the most heat escape; clouds trap some; coldest near sunrise | NWS training page "Dew/Frost" https://www.weather.gov/source/zhu/ZHU_Training_Page/fog_stuff/Dew_Frost/Dew_Frost.htm |
| Water vapor is an invisible gas; humidity is how much is in the air; warm air can hold more | NOAA SciJinks https://scijinks.gov/rain |
| Dew: vapor condenses on surfaces cooled below the dew point. Frost: on surfaces at or below freezing, vapor deposits straight into ice | NWS "Dew/Frost" page above |
| Clouds are tiny droplets; droplets collide and grow until too heavy and fall as rain | NOAA SciJinks "What makes it rain" https://scijinks.gov/about/k-12-education/atmosphere/what-makes-it-rain |
| Ice crystals in cold clouds grow into six-armed snowflakes | NOAA SciJinks |
| Snow melting in a warm layer reaches the ground as rain; much mid-latitude rain starts as snow | UBC ATSC 303 lecture notes https://www.eoas.ubc.ca/courses/atsc303/Lectures/PrMet2017-Precipitation.pdf |

## Simplifications
- Water vapor is drawn as dots; in real life you can't see it (said on screen).
- "Warm air can hold more water vapor" is the usual kids' simplification of saturation vapor pressure rising with temperature.
- Sizes and distances in the space and water-cycle shots are not to scale.
- The thermometer shows warm/cold only, no numbers.
